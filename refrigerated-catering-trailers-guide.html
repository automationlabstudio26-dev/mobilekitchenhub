Cold-chain builder guide

Plan a refrigerated catering trailer that protects product and power margin

A mobile cold store must hold temperature through hot weather, door openings, travel and power changes—without exceeding electrical or payload limits. Prepare a scope that refrigeration specialists and trailer fabricators can price consistently.

Cooling-load brief

Give the designer more than trailer dimensions

Capacity should be selected from a documented refrigeration-load calculation. A generic “BTU per square foot” shortcut can miss the conditions that dominate a mobile installation.

Product duty

State whether the unit only holds pre-chilled goods or must pull warm product down to temperature. Record product type, mass, loading pattern and recovery time.

Ambient design

Provide the hottest and coldest expected operating conditions, solar exposure, event duration, travel profile and whether the trailer will operate indoors or outdoors.

Envelope

Specify dimensions, insulation type and thickness, thermal bridges, floor construction, vapor control, door size, seals and service penetrations.

Infiltration

Door-opening frequency, loading periods, strip curtains or air barriers and staff workflow can materially change heat and moisture entering the space.

Internal gains

Include lights, evaporator fans, people, defrost, monitoring hardware and any equipment operating inside the conditioned volume.

Temperature zones

Chilled, frozen and preparation areas require different equipment and controls. Identify every zone, setpoint and acceptable operating range.

Holding vs. pull-down

Do not confuse storage with rapid cooling

A holding system is intended to maintain already-cold product. Pulling down warm loads can require materially more capacity and different airflow. Tell the designer how goods arrive and how quickly they must reach the required temperature.

Local compliance

Use the governing temperature rule

For U.S. planning, the FDA Food Code is a model adopted or adapted by jurisdictions; it uses 5°C (41°F) or below for many cold-held time/temperature-control foods. Local rules and product-specific requirements govern. Other countries use their own standards.

Electrical and backup

Design for compressor starting, defrost and loss of supply

Document electrical data

Capture voltage, frequency, phase, running current, maximum input, compressor starting characteristics, defrost load and protective-device requirements from the selected equipment.

Coordinate every source

Shore, generator, battery/inverter and vehicle-driven systems have different limits. Specify transfer arrangements, neutral and earthing requirements, connectors and interlocks.

Plan for failure

Define alarm thresholds, temperature logging, remote notification, backup connection, escalation contacts and the approved response when temperature leaves its safe range.

Weight and operation

Cold storage can consume payload quickly

Insulated panels, reinforced floors, doors, evaporators, condensing equipment, batteries or generators and the stored product all contribute to mass. Water or ice adds more. Calculate the loaded condition and axle reactions—not merely the empty trailer.

Prepare your enquiry

Send a refrigerated-trailer brief a builder can price

  1. Operating region, product type and required temperatures.
  2. Holding-only or pull-down duty, product mass and loading pattern.
  3. Internal dimensions, zones, shelving and access requirements.
  4. Ambient design conditions and door-opening profile.
  5. Shore, generator, battery or vehicle-power preferences.
  6. Monitoring, alarm, record-retention and backup expectations.
  7. Payload, axle, towing, budget, schedule and approval constraints.
