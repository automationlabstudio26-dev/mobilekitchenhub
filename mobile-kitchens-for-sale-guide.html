New build, ready-built stock or used mobile kitchen?

Custom new build

Best when the menu, service volume and workflow require a specific layout. Compare drawings, specifications, milestone payments, approval support, warranties and change-order terms.

New ready-built unit

May shorten delivery time, but only if the installed appliances, payload, service openings and utility systems match your operation. Confirm exactly what the advertised price includes.

Used food truck or trailer

Can reduce the purchase price and lead time. Budget for professional inspections, repairs, equipment replacement, compliance changes, rebranding and lost trading time.

Pre-purchase inspection

What to inspect before buying a mobile kitchen

Identity, ownership and road use

  • Vehicle identification, trailer plate and seller identity
  • Ownership, finance, lien or theft checks where available
  • Registration, roadworthiness and service history
  • Tyres, brakes, suspension, coupling, chassis and corrosion
  • Dimensions, axle ratings, payload and towing compatibility

Kitchen shell and hygiene

  • Water ingress, floor condition, seals and structural repairs
  • Cleanable walls, ceilings, counters and junctions
  • Lighting, storage, pest exclusion and service openings
  • Safe access, exits, working aisle and equipment restraint
  • Evidence of previous inspection issues or modifications

Equipment and utilities

  • Manufacturer, model, serial number and data plate for every appliance
  • Operating test under realistic electrical or gas load
  • Electrical panel, shore connector, generator or battery documentation
  • Gas installation, cylinders, ventilation and safety records
  • Fresh water, hot water, wastewater, pumps and leak tests

Ventilation, fire and refrigeration

  • Hood coverage, grease filters, duct condition and exhaust discharge
  • Fire-suppression coverage, service status and interlocks
  • Refrigeration pull-down, seals, drainage and temperature records
  • Maintenance access and availability of replacement parts
  • Professional review of systems affected by your intended menu
Document pack

Ask the seller for evidence—not verbal assurances

Technical records

  • As-built layout and equipment schedule
  • Appliance manuals and warranty records
  • Electrical test and circuit documentation
  • Gas, ventilation and fire-system records
  • Water-system and refrigeration service history

Commercial records

  • Itemised sale agreement and included accessories
  • Deposit, inspection and refund conditions
  • Delivery, collection and insurance responsibilities
  • Known faults, exclusions and seller disclosures
  • Tax invoice and finance settlement evidence
Complete purchase cost

Turn the advertised price into a ready-to-trade budget

Add purchase price, tax, delivery, independent inspections, immediate repairs, appliance replacement, menu-led modifications, approval work, registration, insurance, branding, smallwares, initial stock and contingency. Keep working capital separate so the conversion does not consume the cash needed to open.

Before paying a deposit

Confirm that the unit can legally and commercially fit your plan

  1. Define the intended menu, production volume, staff and trading locations.
  2. Compare the installed equipment against the menu-led equipment schedule.
  3. Calculate electrical load, water demand and payload using verified manufacturer data.
  4. Ask relevant authorities whether plans or inspections are required before modification.
  5. Arrange independent vehicle, trailer and specialist-system inspections.
  6. Price every required correction and agree how discovered defects affect the sale.
  7. Use a written agreement identifying the unit, inclusions, condition and completion terms.

Frequently asked questions

Buying a mobile kitchen

What should I check before buying a used mobile kitchen?

Verify ownership, vehicle or trailer identity, roadworthiness, payload, service history, equipment condition, power, gas, water, ventilation and fire systems, approval documents, warranties and the cost of modifications required for your menu and operating area.

Is a new mobile kitchen better than a used food truck?

A new custom unit can better match a menu and workflow but normally requires more time and capital. A used unit may be available sooner, but condition, compliance evidence and modification costs can outweigh the lower headline price.

Does a food truck for sale include permits?

Do not assume that existing permits transfer to a new owner or operating area. Confirm food-business, vehicle, trading, fire, gas, electrical and site requirements with the relevant authorities before paying a deposit.

How do I compare mobile kitchen sale prices?

Compare the complete delivered and compliant cost: purchase price, tax, delivery, inspections, repairs, modifications, equipment replacement, registration, branding, approvals, finance and downtime—not only the advertised price.
