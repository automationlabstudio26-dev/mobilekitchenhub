Power planning guide

What size generator do I need for a food truck?

A useful starting point is the combined running wattage of every appliance you expect to operate at the same time. The final generator specification must also account for startup current, voltage, electrical phase, ambient conditions, fuel choice, noise limits, and the generator manufacturer’s continuous-duty rating.

Planning formula: add simultaneous running loads, then apply the calculator’s 20% or 35% planning margin. This is a screening estimate—not a substitute for an electrician’s load calculation or a generator supplier’s approval.

1. Build a simultaneous-load list

List cooking, refrigeration, ventilation, air-conditioning, pumps, water heating, lighting, POS equipment, battery chargers, and receptacle loads. Separate equipment that truly runs together from equipment that can be scheduled or interlocked.

2. Check startup and cycling loads

Compressors, pumps, fans, and air conditioners can draw substantially more power while starting than while running. Several motors restarting together after an outage can create a demanding momentary load. Use equipment nameplates and manufacturer data rather than generic internet averages.

3. Confirm voltage and phase

Total watts alone do not prove compatibility. Confirm whether each appliance requires 120 V, 208 V, 240 V, single-phase, or three-phase power, and make sure the generator, distribution panel, connectors, and transfer equipment match the design.

4. Compare generator types

  • Portable/inverter units: potentially useful for smaller loads, but verify continuous output, grounding/bonding instructions, weather protection, and safe placement.
  • Commercial mobile generators: better suited to heavier electrical kitchens and integrated installations, subject to ventilation, exhaust, fuel, and service requirements.
  • Shore power: can reduce generator hours where a compatible, properly rated supply is available.

5. Questions to take to a professional

  • What is the calculated continuous load and largest motor-starting load?
  • Will the design operate at elevation or in high heat?
  • How are neutral, grounding, transfer, overcurrent, and GFCI protection handled?
  • What connectors, cable sizes, fuel storage, exhaust clearances, and local permits apply?
