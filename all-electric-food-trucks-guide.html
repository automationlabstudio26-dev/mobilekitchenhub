All-electric & zero-emission food truck planning

Turn a cooking concept into a realistic load profile, battery and inverter brief—without overlooking charging time, payload, heat rejection or operational backup.

Open the battery calculator
Terminology: an all-electric kitchen has no onboard cooking gas. “Zero-emission” claims depend on the vehicle and boundary being described. Confirm vehicle-zone eligibility and marketing claims independently.

1. Reduce the load first

Choose efficient refrigeration, induction or electric cooking where appropriate, stage heat-up periods and avoid simultaneous peaks that do not support the menu.

2. Separate power from energy

Inverter kW covers simultaneous and surge demand. Battery kWh covers how long the kitchen can run. Both need reserve and conversion-loss allowances.

3. Design charging around service

Confirm depot or commissary supply, connector, available current, dwell time and whether opportunity charging is practical between events.

4. Keep shore power useful

A correctly designed shore connection can support preparation, recharge batteries and reduce cycle depth. Verify the venue supply before relying on it.

5. Budget payload and space

Batteries, enclosures, cooling and power electronics compete with water, stock and equipment for mass and volume. Model axle and GVWR effects early.

6. Plan heat and safety

Coordinate battery-system listings, isolation, protection, ventilation or cooling, emergency response, service access and fire strategy with qualified specialists.

Three common operating architectures

Shore-led kitchen

Venue or commissary supply carries service loads; a smaller battery handles transitions or selected equipment. Best where reliable rated connections are guaranteed.

Battery-led kitchen

The battery and inverter carry most service loads. It needs a disciplined load profile, sufficient usable energy and a dependable recharge window.

Managed hybrid electrical supply

Battery, shore power and another approved supply source are coordinated. Controls must prevent overload and make the operating mode clear to staff.

Build the engineering brief in sequence

  1. Define menu, throughput and operating hours.
  2. Record every appliance's voltage, running power and surge or heat-up behavior.
  3. Create realistic simultaneous-use scenarios.
  4. Size inverter output and distribution protection.
  5. Estimate usable battery energy and recharge time.
  6. Check battery, water and equipment mass against axle, payload and vehicle limits.
  7. Have qualified electrical, vehicle and fire professionals approve the final design.
