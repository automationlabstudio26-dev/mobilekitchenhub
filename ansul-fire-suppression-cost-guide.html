Fire-safety cost guide

Food truck fire-suppression cost: compare the scope, not just the total

A credible quote must be based on your actual appliance lineup, hood and duct, shutdown controls, local approval process and installation conditions. This guide helps you request like-for-like prices without inventing a universal figure.

Build my quote scope
Safety first: This is planning information, not a system design. Use a qualified fire-suppression contractor and obtain approval from the authority having jurisdiction before fabrication or appliance changes.
Price drivers

What changes the final quote

Protected cooking hazards

Appliance type, model, dimensions, fuel and position determine the required coverage. Fryers, griddles, ranges, broilers and woks are not interchangeable hazards.

Hood, plenum and duct

Coverage may extend beyond the appliance line. Hood geometry, plenum and duct arrangement must be coordinated with the listed system and installation instructions.

Agent, cylinders and nozzles

The protected hazard and design method affect agent capacity, cylinder count, piping, fittings and nozzle selection. Ask for the proposed design basis.

Detection and manual release

Detection components, control hardware, manual pull stations and routing all add material and installation work—especially inside a compact vehicle.

Fuel and electrical shutdowns

System actuation may need to stop fuel or electrical energy to protected appliances and coordinate alarms or ventilation. Clarify who supplies every interlock.

Approval and lifecycle scope

Drawings, permits, inspection attendance, commissioning, travel, certification records and future inspection or service can be priced separately.

      Design choice

      Appliance-specific coverage

      Nozzles are selected and positioned for defined appliances. This can be precise, but changing a model or moving equipment may alter the approved coverage.

      Design choice

      Overlapping coverage

      Some listed designs create overlapping protection zones for greater layout flexibility within defined limits. It still requires a compliant design and contractor review.

      Before accepting a quote

      Seven items to confirm in writing

      1. The exact appliances and hazards covered.
      2. The proposed listed system and design method.
      3. All hood, duct, piping, nozzle, detection and release inclusions.
      4. Required gas and electrical shutdowns and who completes them.
      5. Drawings, permit fees, inspection attendance and commissioning.
      6. Training, completion records, warranties and future service terms.
      7. How appliance or layout changes will be reviewed and priced.
