Four routes

How the options differ

Buy an existing unit

Best fit: Faster entry when the layout and equipment already suit the menu.

  • Inspect condition, payload, service history and corrosion
  • Verify ownership, registration and approval documents
  • Price modifications and obsolete equipment
  • Test electrical, gas, water, ventilation and refrigeration systems

Commission a custom build

Best fit: Long-term operation requiring a specific workflow, equipment set or brand.

  • Prepare a menu-led technical brief before quotations
  • Compare inclusions, milestones, warranties and change terms
  • Confirm inspection and handover documentation
  • Allow design, approval, fabrication and correction time

Hire or rent

Best fit: Events, temporary contracts, renovation cover, seasonal work or concept testing.

  • Confirm delivery, installation and collection charges
  • Check utility, site-access and generator requirements
  • Review cleaning, damage and breakdown responsibilities
  • Confirm the equipment list and local approvals

Lease or finance

Best fit: Spreading payments when a suitable asset and acceptable terms are available.

  • Compare total payments, deposit and end-of-term ownership
  • Check modification, mileage and location restrictions
  • Allocate maintenance, insurance and compliance duties
  • Review guarantees, default and early-exit terms professionally
True-cost comparison

Put every quotation on the same basis

Upfront

Deposit, purchase price, delivery, tax, registration, inspections, initial equipment, branding and modifications.

Operating

Monthly payments, insurance, maintenance, site fees, storage, fuel, utilities, cleaning and compliance renewals.

Exit and downtime

Resale value, removal, restoration, early termination, replacement-unit cover and revenue lost during breakdown or modification.

Pre-decision checks

Do not commit until the unit fits the operation

  • The dimensions, payload and service openings suit the intended sites
  • The equipment supports the menu and required service volume
  • The power, gas, ventilation, water and wastewater systems are documented
  • The unit can meet the approval requirements in every intended area
  • The agreement identifies maintenance, breakdown and insurance responsibilities
  • The total cost remains viable under realistic sales assumptions

Frequently asked questions

Buying, building, hiring and leasing

Is it better to buy or build a food truck?

Buying an existing unit can reduce lead time, while a custom build can better match a specific menu and workflow. The right choice depends on condition, compliance evidence, equipment fit, budget and the cost of modifications.

Can I lease a food truck?

Lease and finance availability varies by supplier, location, business history and asset type. Compare the total payments, deposit, ownership terms, mileage, modification limits, insurance, maintenance and early-exit conditions.

When should I hire a mobile kitchen?

Hiring can suit short contracts, events, renovation cover, seasonal demand or testing a concept before purchasing. Confirm delivery, utilities, equipment, approvals, cleaning and damage terms.

Mobile Kitchen Hub does not sell, hire, lease or finance vehicles. This guide is general planning information. Verify the unit, agreement, tax treatment, finance, insurance and local approval requirements with appropriate independent professionals.
