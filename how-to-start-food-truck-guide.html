How to start a food truck or mobile food van business

Move from menu idea to an authority-ready, builder-ready plan without choosing the vehicle or equipment too early.

Recommended sequence

Nine steps from concept to opening

1. Choose the customer and location

Define whether you will serve streets, offices, private events, markets, festivals or contracted sites. Record the cities and venues you intend to use.

2. Design a serviceable menu

List ingredients, preparation steps, cooking methods, holding times and expected orders per hour. A smaller, repeatable launch menu is easier to equip and operate.

3. Build the operating budget

Separate vehicle or trailer, conversion, equipment, professional design, permits, insurance, site fees, ingredients, labour, fuel, utilities, maintenance and contingency.

4. Compare buy, build, hire and lease

Match the ownership model to your contract length, available capital, menu, branding and technical requirements.

5. Check approvals before construction

Ask the responsible food-safety, trading, planning, fire, vehicle, gas and electrical authorities for their current requirements and application sequence.

6. Prepare the technical brief

Create a scaled layout, equipment schedule, workflow, water plan, ventilation scope and preliminary electrical load using model-specific appliance data.

7. Compare itemised quotations

Ask fabricators to identify equipment, materials, exclusions, testing, approvals, warranties, delivery, payment stages and change-control terms.

8. Inspect and document the unit

Complete applicable health, fire, gas, electrical, vehicle and site checks. Collect test records, manuals, serial numbers and as-built schedules.

9. Test the operation

Run a controlled service before a major event. Test preparation time, peak power, refrigeration recovery, water capacity, waste handling and cleaning.

Core numbers

Calculate the business before calculating the vehicle

Sales capacity

Estimate realistic orders per hour, average order value, operating hours and trading days. Stress-test quiet days, weather and event cancellations.

Food and labour

Cost every menu item, packaging and payment fee. Model staffing for preparation, travel, setup, service, cleaning and administration—not service hours alone.

Ownership and operating cost

Include finance or hire payments, insurance, site fees, fuel, generator servicing, vehicle maintenance, storage, commissary costs and renewals.

Technical planning

Turn the menu into a build brief

Each appliance affects space, weight, power or gas, ventilation, fire protection, refrigeration, water and workflow. Choose actual models before finalising the layout and use manufacturer data rather than generic wattage alone.

Approvals

Registration is not always permission to trade

Food-business registration or licensing can be separate from street-vending, market, event, landowner, planning, vehicle, fire, gas and electrical requirements. Confirm each intended operating location before launch.

Frequently asked questions

Starting a mobile food business

What is the first step in starting a food truck business?

Define the customer, trading locations, menu, service volume and operating model before selecting a vehicle. These decisions determine equipment, utilities, permits, staffing and budget.

Should I buy a food truck before applying for permits?

Check the responsible authorities' current plan-review, equipment and construction requirements before committing to a vehicle or non-refundable build deposit.

How do I calculate food truck equipment power?

Collect each appliance's rated voltage, phase, current or watts, identify which loads run together, account for manufacturer startup data, and have the final design confirmed by a competent professional.

This guide is general planning information, not legal, regulatory, financial or food-safety advice. Costs and requirements vary by business model and location. Verify current requirements with the responsible authorities and qualified professionals.
