Food truck electrical panels: 50 amp vs. 100 amp

The correct service size comes from a documented load calculation and the way the kitchen will actually operate. The “50 amp” or “100 amp” label alone does not tell you the available power unless voltage, conductors, connector configuration, and phase are also defined.

Avoid shortcut math. Two services with the same amp rating can have different usable capacity. Have the builder or electrician document the voltage, phase, conductor arrangement, continuous loads, demand assumptions, and largest startup load.

When a smaller service may work

A more modest panel may suit a menu that uses propane for major cooking loads and electricity mainly for refrigeration, ventilation, pumps, lighting, and small appliances. Load management may be necessary to prevent high-demand appliances from operating together.

When more capacity may be needed

All-electric cooking, electric water heating, multiple refrigeration compressors, rooftop air-conditioning, espresso equipment, or future expansion can quickly increase demand. Extra panel capacity is useful only if the generator, shore connection, feeders, and upstream protection can supply it.

Shore power and generator coordination

  • Confirm the inlet and cable rating, conductor count, locking configuration, and weather protection.
  • Use listed transfer equipment where more than one source can feed the truck.
  • Verify neutral and equipment-grounding arrangements for every operating mode.
  • Label circuits and provide a load schedule that matches the finished installation.

Protection near a working kitchen

Mobile kitchens combine electricity, metal surfaces, water, heat, vibration, and outdoor exposure. Circuit protection, GFCI requirements, equipment listing, cable routing, enclosure ratings, bonding, and inspection rules must be determined by qualified professionals and local authorities.

Builder questions

  • What loads were included in the panel calculation?
  • Which appliances require dedicated circuits?
  • Can the generator and shore inlet support the full panel rating?
  • Is there documented spare capacity for planned equipment?
  • Who will provide electrical drawings and inspection documentation?
